# Product

## One sentence

**Capitol League is fantasy sports for Congress.**

Users draft real legislators and score points from real congressional activity.

## The hook

Ownership changes attention.

Most people will not routinely inspect congressional roll calls.

But if a legislator is on their fantasy team, a missed vote suddenly matters.

The desired reaction is:

> "Why the hell did three of my picks not show up today?"

That is the product.

## First session

Target: **playing in under 60 seconds.**

A user can either:

- deliberately draft a roster, or
- press **Random Draft** and get a valid team immediately.

No political expertise should be required.

## Return session

The main screen should behave like a sports box score.

Example:

```text
MY TEAM                 184

7 players scored
2 had no activity
3 missed votes

BEST
+32  Bill advanced

WORST
 -8  Missed 4 of 6 votes

MATCHUP
You      184
Brandon  201
```

Then the player can tap any event to see the underlying vote, bill, or official source.

## Product rule

**Fantasy first. Understanding Congress happens naturally afterward.**

Surface-level scoring should feel obvious:

- show up = good
- miss work = bad
- do meaningful legislative work = good
- move legislation forward = bigger play
- major legislative success = big play

The underlying model may need normalization for chamber, role, committee access, and opportunity.

The explanation shown to the player should stay simple.

## Tone

Borrow from fantasy sports:

- player cards
- sleepers
- hot/cold streaks
- matchup recaps
- roster regret
- weekly winners/losers
- attendance/no-show penalties
- "big play" legislative events

Avoid turning the main experience into a civics curriculum.

## Neutrality

Capitol League should score observable actions rather than political ideology.

A point-moving event should be traceable to source data.

The game can show *what happened* without deciding whether a political viewpoint is correct.
