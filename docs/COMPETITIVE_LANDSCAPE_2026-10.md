# Competitive Landscape — October 2026

This document is a dated market snapshot, not a permanent claim.

## Direct competitors

### Fantasy Government

Website: https://www.fantasygovernment.com/

Current positioning:

- Draft real Senators and Representatives
- Score real legislative activity
- Public/private fantasy mechanics
- Legislator profiles and live scoring
- Non-partisan/activity-based framing
- Free tier plus paid Civics Fan tier
- Strong civics/education positioning

The public site is actively showing 119th Congress activity.

### Politiparty

Website: https://politiparty.com/

Current positioning:

- "Box scores for Congress"
- Draft 3 Senators + 5 Representatives
- Captain multiplier
- Weekly competition
- Open/public scoring rubric
- Attendance penalties
- Bills, amendments, votes, and legislative progression
- Every point tied to official public records
- Explicitly neutral/non-ideological framing

The public site is actively scoring current Congress.

## Older / adjacent products

### Fantasy Congress

Website: https://fantasycongress.com/

A long-running version of the basic fantasy-Congress concept. Important primarily as evidence that the concept predates the current 2026 wave.

### Fantasy Elect

Website: https://fantasyelect.io/

More prediction-contest oriented than roster-based fantasy Congress. Treat as adjacent rather than a direct game-loop competitor.

## Traction assessment

As of this snapshot, public evidence confirms active products but does **not** establish that either Fantasy Government or Politiparty has won the market.

Publicly visible evidence did not provide reliable large-scale:

- user counts
- revenue
- league counts
- funding totals
- dominant community scale

That means the category is validated enough to have multiple current builders, but no publicly obvious category winner is established.

## What competitors validate

The following loop is no longer novel by itself:

> Draft members of Congress -> score legislative activity -> compete.

That is market validation, but it is not differentiation.

## Capitol League differentiation target

Capitol League should emphasize consumer fantasy behavior rather than leading with education:

- one-click/random roster onboarding
- under-one-minute first play
- 30-second daily/weekly box scores
- immediate attendance/no-show consequences
- matchup emotion
- sports-style player presentation
- roster regret / sleepers / streaks / trending players
- simple "why did I gain/lose points?" drill-down
- source-backed evidence behind every material event

The product principle:

> **A game that accidentally teaches the user what Congress is doing.**

Rather than:

> **A civics product with a fantasy-game wrapper.**

## Paid fantasy / wagering

Missouri's legalization of sports wagering makes monetization/regulatory analysis worth revisiting, but that is a **separate legal/product workstream**.

Do not assume that a political fantasy contest, paid fantasy league, sportsbook wager, or political event contract shares the same regulatory classification.

The core game should be designed to work without money first.
