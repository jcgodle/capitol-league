# Product Direction — Quick, Easy, Meaningful

## Product thesis

Capitol League should feel like checking fantasy football, not studying a civics textbook.

A person should be able to:

1. Sign up.
2. Draft a roster — or press **Random Draft**.
3. Leave.
4. Come back after Congress acts.
5. Immediately understand whether their team had a good or terrible day.

The emotional hook is **ownership**.

A user does not need to care about a legislator before drafting them. Once that legislator is on the user's team, missed votes and productive actions suddenly have personal consequences.

## Desired first-session experience

Target: **playing in under 60 seconds.**

A first-time user who knows almost nothing about Congress should be able to press:

> Random Draft

and receive a valid roster.

No research prerequisite. No civics quiz. No requirement to recognize names.

## Desired return experience

The home screen should function as a sports box score.

Example:

```text
MY TEAM                184 pts today

7 members scored
2 had no activity
3 missed votes

TOP PLAYER
+32  Bill advanced from committee

WORST PLAYER
 -8  Missed 4 of 6 votes

MATCHUP
You: 184
Brandon: 201
```

The next question is naturally:

> Why did this guy cost me eight points?

That click opens the relevant votes, bills, attendance record, and official sources.

That is where the education happens.

## Design rule

**Fantasy first. Understanding Congress happens as a consequence of caring about the fantasy team.**

Do not require the user to understand legislative procedure to interpret the score.

At the surface level:

- Show up = good
- Miss work = bad
- Do legislative work = good
- Move legislation forward = bigger play
- Get something enacted = major play
- Do something unusual/noteworthy = bonus, badge, or event

The underlying model can be sophisticated enough to balance House/Senate, leadership, committees, session schedules, and opportunity differences.

The user-facing explanation should remain simple.

## The 30-second habit

The retention target is a daily/weekly check:

> What did my team do?

Not:

> What happened in Congress today?

Those become the same question only after the user owns a roster.

## Product personality

Capitol League should borrow heavily from sports products:

- player cards
- matchup scores
- daily/weekly recaps
- streaks
- sleepers
- trending players
- high/low performers
- "big play" events
- roster mistakes
- bench decisions
- attendance/no-show penalties
- matchup consequences

The tone can have personality without taking ideological positions.

## Political neutrality

The scoring system should grade observable activity, not political beliefs.

Every point-moving event should be traceable to a source record.

That makes the game explainable and reduces pressure for Capitol League to decide which political outcomes are morally or ideologically "correct."

## What this is not

The 2026 restart should not begin as:

- an online civics curriculum
- a bill encyclopedia
- a prediction market
- a sportsbook
- a comprehensive congressional-data warehouse

Those may be adjacent opportunities later.

The first job is to make the roster emotionally sticky.
